Web Development

Keeping a PHP or CMS Website Secure and Up to Date

A website is often the public face of an organisation, and an unpatched or poorly configured site is an easy target. Most successful attacks use well-known weaknesses that were fixed long ago.

A good first step is to keep the CMS, themes, plugins and PHP version updated. After that, store passwords with modern hashing and never in plain text, and protect admin pages with strong logins and limited attempts. Just as important, back up the site and database on a regular schedule. Finally, enable HTTPS everywhere and set secure cookie options.

My hands-on work as ASSISTANT HRIO at Rexus Medical Centre has taught me to favour simple, repeatable habits over complicated tools. Treat updates and backups as routine maintenance, and most threats lose their chance before they begin.

Cybersecurity

Why Giving Everyone Admin Access Is a Hidden Risk

Zero trust sounds complicated, but the idea is simple: never assume a person or device is safe just because it is inside the network. Every request should be checked, and every user should only reach what they need.

Start by deciding to separate administrator accounts from everyday user accounts. It also helps to review who has access to what at least twice a year and to require multi-factor authentication for anything sensitive. Over time, remove access immediately when someone changes role or leaves, and log important actions so unusual activity can be spotted.

My hands-on work as ASSISTANT HRIO at Rexus Medical Centre has taught me to favour simple, repeatable habits over complicated tools. Begin with the most sensitive systems and expand gradually; perfection is not required to make a real difference.
